## the moment of truth

a graph is links between particles; a link outlives its meaning only if the particle itself survives. so we audited the [[cybernode]] blockstore directly, file by file, all 3,143,648 particles:

    3,069,134 present — 97.63%

five years of collective knowledge, a dead chain, and the content is still there, servable, block by block. the missing 74,513 are published as a list — nothing swept under the rug. this is what content addressing was always for: the chain died, the knowledge did not

## why altruism worked

the strange part: there was never an economic incentive to store any of this. the protocol charged for linking — bandwidth priced in [[H]] — and paid nothing for hosting. by token-engineering logic the content should be gone. it is 97.63% here. three reasons:

- content addressing makes altruism cheap. a [[particle]] is its hash; keeping it is a disk, not a service contract. four terabytes of collective knowledge costs less than a dinner per month to hold — the [[cybernode]] simply pinned what the graph referenced, for years, because the operator cared about the graph more than about the margin
- the address never rots. an altruist who stores a particle today makes it recoverable by anyone forever — effort compounds instead of evaporating, which is exactly backwards from URL hosting where neglect deletes history
- the missing 2.37% tells the honest limit: particles that were linked but never uploaded to the node that cared — hashes whose bytes lived only on a laptop that left. altruism preserves what it can see

incentive design still matters — but the bootloader measured the baseline: one motivated archivist plus content addressing retains ~98% of a dead network's knowledge. that is the floor the new economics builds on
