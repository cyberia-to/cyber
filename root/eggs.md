Eggs are reproductive bodies laid by [[birds]], [[reptiles]], and [[fish]], with [[chicken]] eggs the most widely consumed worldwide.
A single egg delivers complete [[protein]], [[choline]], [[vitamin D]], [[B12]], and essential [[fatty acids]] in a compact, bioavailable form.

The [[yolk]] carries fat-soluble [[vitamins]] and [[lecithin]], while the [[albumen]] (egg white) is nearly pure protein and [[water]].
In [[cooking]], eggs function as leavening agents, emulsifiers, binders, and a standalone ingredient across countless [[recipes]].

Pastured hens that forage on [[grass]] and [[insects]] produce eggs with deeper-colored yolks and elevated [[omega-3]] content.
Egg quality reflects the health of the [[soil]], the diversity of the hen's [[diet]], and the welfare conditions of the flock.

Preserved forms include [[pickled eggs]], [[salted eggs]], and century eggs, each relying on [[fermentation]] or [[curing]] chemistry.
Eggs connect the cycles of [[animal husbandry]], [[composting]], and [[garden]] fertility in an integrated [[homestead]] system.
