verifiable evidence. a [[hash]] proves measurement, a [[cyberlink]] proves relevance, spent [[focus]] proves commitment, [[finality]] proves [[consensus]]

[[proof]] is the mechanism by which [[cyber]] establishes trust without intermediaries. every claim in the protocol rests on a verifiable chain of evidence.

a [[hash]] is a proof of existence — it demonstrates that specific [[data]] was measured and assigned a [[particle]] identity. anyone can recompute the [[hash]] and verify the match.

a [[cyberlink]] is a proof of [[relevance]]. when a [[neuron]] links two [[particles]], it stakes its [[focus]] on the claim that a meaningful relationship exists between them.

spent [[focus]] is a proof of commitment. [[neurons]] allocate finite [[bandwidth]] to create [[cyberlinks]], making every link an economic act with real cost. the scarcer the [[focus]], the stronger the signal.

[[finality]] is a proof of [[consensus]]. once a [[block]] is finalized, the [[cyberlinks]] within it are accepted by the entire network as canonical entries in the [[knowledge graph]].

[[Merkle proofs]] allow lightweight verification. a [[neuron]] can confirm that a specific [[cyberlink]] or [[transaction]] exists in a finalized [[block]] without downloading the entire chain.

the layered proof architecture — existence, relevance, commitment, consensus — gives [[cyber]] its epistemic foundation. every [[particle]], every link, every state transition is independently verifiable.
