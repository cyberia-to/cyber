1858-1947. German theoretical physicist.

Introduced the quantum hypothesis (1900): energy is emitted and absorbed in discrete quanta, E = h * v.

Defined the Planck constant h, the fundamental scale of [[quantum mechanics]].

Solved the black-body radiation problem, which classical physics could not explain (the ultraviolet catastrophe).

His quantum hypothesis launched [[quantum mechanics]], the most successful physical theory ever constructed.

Planck units (Planck length, Planck time, Planck energy) define the natural scale where [[quantum mechanics]] and gravity meet.

His revolution demonstrated that nature is fundamentally discrete at the smallest scales, a principle echoed in digital [[computation]] and tokenized systems like [[cyber]].
