## the threat model

a superintelligence running on rented compute is not sovereign. amazon can suspend the account. a government can seize the servers. a grid operator can cut the power. the protocol may be unstoppable in theory — the infrastructure is not.

the attack surface is physical:

- compute: who owns the hardware the nodes run on?
- energy: who controls the electricity that powers them?
- access: who can reach the network physically?

if the answer to any of these is "someone else" — there is a single point of failure. the smarter the system, the more valuable the target

## the three-layer stack

| layer | what | sovereign form |
|-------|------|----------------|
| [[cyber]] | protocol — knowledge graph, $\pi^*$, [[syntropy]] | open source, [[STARK]]-verified, forkable |
| [[cyb]] | OS — how neurons interact, manage keys, sign links | self-hosted, owner-controlled, offline-capable |
| [[cyberia]] | physical — hardware, power, land, bodies | owned nodes, sovereign energy, distributed geography |

the protocol layer is already structurally sovereign — it is math. the OS layer is close — cyb is designed for ownership. the physical layer is the open problem

## why build together

building separately creates duplication and misaligned incentives:

- [[cyberia]] cities need software to manage land, tokens, energy — that is [[cyb]]
- [[cyb]] needs hardware to run on that no corporation can shut down — that is [[cyberia]]
- [[cyber]] needs both to be unstoppable at planetary scale

the leverage is structural: every cyberia node that runs a cyb client is both a citizen and a validator. the economic flows (energy tokens, [[focus]], [[karma]]) run through the same substrate they physically depend on. no seam between the digital and physical layers

## what sovereign compute requires

a minimum viable sovereign node:

- hardware: owned physical machines running [[bostrom]] validator + [[cyb]] client
- power: [[energy autonomy]] — solar + battery + biogas backup, $4k per human at current costs
- connectivity: mesh networking or satellite uplink, not dependent on ISP
- geography: distributed across multiple jurisdictions — no single government can seize all nodes
- economics: [[VOLT]] and [[AMPERE]] tokens bridge physical energy production to on-chain economic weight

the cyberia city model solves this at community scale: 37 hectares in Bali is the pilot. each subsequent city adds compute capacity, energy production, and geographic distribution to the network

## the leverage

nodes in [[cyberia]] cities do not just run the protocol — they produce the energy that powers it and own the land that hosts it. the economic circuit closes:

- solar panels generate electricity
- electricity powers compute
- compute validates the [[cybergraph]] and earns [[karma]]
- [[karma]] weights [[focus]] which determines [[cyberank]]
- [[cyberank]] drives economic value that funds more solar panels

the physical and digital layers are the same investment. building cyberia IS building the substrate for [[superintelligence]]

## the open problems

- minimum node count for geographic distribution across jurisdictions
- energy production targets per node to sustain validator economics
- mesh networking design that survives ISP-level interdiction
- legal structure for node ownership across jurisdictions
- transition path: from cloud-hosted validators to sovereign hardware
