# 3C — cross-cell communication

one protocol for every rung of the [[cell]] ladder: organ to organ, building to building, [[research/oikos|household]] to household, shard to shard — and to external chains, which are just cells whose proofs are weaker. three verbs, and none of them is *transport*:

| mode | what crosses | mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **read** | proofs of foreign state | [[zheng]]-verified header chains; answers carry a tier ([[cyb/parts/state|state]]: T0 proof · T1 anchor · T3 badged) |
| **write** | proofs of our state | [[focus]] summaries, [[cyberank]] attestations, any graph fact exported with a proof of correct computation |
| **trade** | conditions and receipts | [[research/oikos|oikos]]: programmed conditions on home ledgers, matched by epoch, settled pairwise |

**no tokens ever move.** there is no bridge mode, no wrapping, no custodial mint/burn — not deprecated but absent: under [[research/oikos|oikos]] a transfer is a rule on the token's home cell ("balance moves to X upon proof of event E, at tier ≥ τ, before deadline t"), and the largest exploit class in crypto history becomes unexpressible

## the insight, completed

cross-[[cell]] communication and cross-chain communication were always the same problem: verify that a state transition on the other side was valid, without trusting the other side's validators. what kept them apart was token transport — the one act that demanded a trusted middle. remove it, and the unification is literal: one relay of proofs serves a robot's organ asking a building for a receipt and a household settling a trade with another household

## read — proofs in

a cell tracks a counterparty by verifying its consensus as a [[nox]] program: every header transition proven, N headers folded into one recursive [[zheng]] proof, verification O(1) regardless of depth. no honest-majority assumption about foreign validators — the proof guarantees the rules were followed, whoever ran them. freshness is explicit: every read carries `at` and a tier, and conditions state the tier they require. the economics of keeping counterparty proofs fresh is a market — [[network]] §paid headers

## write — proofs out

any external system can ask the [[cybergraph]]: the answer is a ranked subgraph with a [[zheng]] proof that the ranking was computed correctly from authenticated state. the focus oracle is an export of knowledge, priced in the same header market

## trade — conditions, never value

the two open protocols of [[research/oikos|oikos]] live here:

1. **matching without a free option** — order *commitments* (information, not tokens) post to the routing cell; legs bind to the same [[foculus]] epoch beacon: both sides' conditions activate in one epoch or neither does. [[communication]]'s proof-of-delivery extends to trade legs: a leg is not committed until its delivery to the counterparty is proven
2. **the freshness contract** — every condition names its tier and expiry. a proof from a halted counterparty ages out; nothing dangles. [[bostrom]]'s burial is the empirical case study of what a dead chain's proof is worth

## registration — the routing table

a cell enters the mesh by linking its name and state root into the [[cybergraph]]. the graph is the routing table: resolve a name, get a root, demand a proof. one hop gives any cell a T1 anchor on any other — the mesh of mutual anchoring that makes ten thousand small cells safe
