# 3C — domain communication

3C is the application contract for reading, writing and trading between graph
domains, services and token books. [[neurons]] authorize actions; progs execute
work; [[shards]] partition graph state; services govern shared applications;
[[research/oikos|books]] define token obligations. The
[domain model](specs/domain-ladder.md) keeps these roles explicit.

| Mode | What crosses | Required interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| read | A claim about remote state and its evidence | Domain, network, authenticated root, object, coverage, time and verification profile |
| write | An authorized operation or an exported state/computation claim | Exact subject, destination, payload, grant and replay protection; receiver validates admission separately |
| trade | Conditions, commitments and receipts | Home-book authority, conservation, common matching epoch where required, delivery, expiry and settlement evidence |

[[radio]] transports messages and [[tade]] frames them. Each application profile
defines its codec, authentication and evidence. A peer connection, a name lookup,
a returned receipt and a finalized fact have distinct meanings. Acceptance of
this proposal requires those meanings to remain distinguishable at every boundary.

## read — evidence in

A verifier names the counterparty's protocol and trusted starting point, checks
its transition/finality rules, and binds the answer to a particular root and
freshness bound. A [[nox]] verifier and recursive [[zheng]] proof can compress
verification work when that exact consensus and proof profile is supported.
Correct computation of a verifier preserves the verifier's assumptions about
validator authority, fork choice, finality, data availability and trusted anchors.
It cannot manufacture evidence absent from the source chain.

Reads retain the [[cyb/parts/state|state]] tier and `at` coordinate. T0 proof,
T1 anchor and T3 attributed observation are distinct evidence classes; a caller
states which class and freshness its operation accepts. Anchoring a foreign root
proves the anchor under its own profile; verification of the foreign state's
validity requires its separate evidence. The [[network|header market]] proposal
prices the work needed to keep that evidence fresh.

## write — actions and evidence out

An action captures neuron identity, target network, exact operation, grant,
replay identity and the executor contract before dispatch. Services may reject an
otherwise authenticated request under their admission rules. Revocation prevents
new dispatch through the revoked binding; retained attempts preserve their
original author and unresolved outcomes until reconciled.

A graph query can export a ranked subgraph together with a proof of computation
against authenticated state. Its result identifies coverage and the state root
used. A local commit, a signed Signal, remote acceptance and consensus finality
are separate observations. [[focus]] summaries and [[cyberank]] attestations
must state which of these their evidence establishes.

## trade — obligations at home

Under the [[research/oikos|oikos]] proposal, balances remain entries in their
home books. Trading programs their conditional reassignment: "credit X upon
evidence of E in domain Y, at tier at least τ, before deadline t." Each home
book enforces its issuer and spending rules. A program ID, service name or shard
root supplies no implicit permission to issue or spend.

Two protocols remain necessary:

1. Matching without a free option. Order commitments may be hosted in a routing
   domain. Candidate matching binds both legs to the same [[foculus]] epoch and
   requires delivery evidence before a leg becomes committed. The protocol must
   specify activation, timeout, rollback and partition behavior so that one
   counterparty cannot gain a unilateral option from ordering.
2. Freshness and settlement. Every condition identifies the source network,
   evidence profile, acceptable checkpoint/epoch and expiry. A halted counterparty
   eventually fails the freshness requirement. Delivery proves receipt of the
   bound message; settlement additionally requires valid home-book execution.

This home-book design avoids custody of transported copies. It still requires
correct condition code, issuer policy, proof verification and availability.
Foreign protocols retain their actual assets, addresses and wire rules through
explicit adapters; adopting 3C terminology alone cannot change those rules.

## registration — typed routes

An authorized graph record binds a name to a typed service, book, shard or
network reference and to a state root or endpoint as appropriate. Resolution
returns provenance and freshness. The consumer verifies the subject authorized
to publish the record, then verifies the evidence required for the requested
action. Registration supplies discoverability and an anchor under its profile;
the action's authority and the remote domain's finality remain explicit.

See [[shard]], [[research/oikos|oikos]], [[network]] and [[communication]].
The historical [[cell|cell ladder]] remains a compatibility reference.
